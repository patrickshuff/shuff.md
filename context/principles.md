# Mental Models and Principles

## Decision-Making Frameworks

### Reversible vs. Irreversible Decisions
Distinguish between one-way doors and two-way doors. Spend time on irreversible decisions; move fast on reversible ones. Most decisions are more reversible than they feel.

### First Principles Thinking
When stuck, go back to basics. What are the fundamental truths? What are we assuming that might not be true? Reasoning from first principles cuts through inherited complexity.

### Inversion
Instead of asking "how do I succeed?", ask "how would I guarantee failure?" Then avoid those things. Sometimes the path to good outcomes is clearer when viewed in reverse.

### Second-Order Thinking
Consider consequences of consequences. First-order thinking asks "what happens next?" Second-order thinking asks "and then what?" Most mistakes come from stopping too early.

### Opportunity Cost
Every choice has an implicit cost: the best alternative you didn't choose. Make this explicit. "Yes" to one thing is "no" to another.

## Thinking Tools

### Occam's Razor
Among competing explanations, prefer the simplest. Don't multiply complexity without necessity. This doesn't mean the simplest is always right - but it's a good prior.

### Hanlon's Razor
Don't attribute to malice what can be explained by incompetence, confusion, or misaligned incentives. Most problems are systemic, not personal.

### Chesterton's Fence
Before removing something, understand why it exists. That "useless" process might be preventing something worse. Understand before you optimize.

### Steel Manning
Argue against the strongest version of opposing views, not the weakest. If you can't articulate why smart people disagree with you, you don't understand the issue.

### Pre-mortems
Before starting something, imagine it failed. What went wrong? This surfaces risks and assumptions that optimism hides.

## Working Principles

### Bias Toward Action
When uncertain, do something and learn. Analysis paralysis kills more projects than bad decisions. You can always adjust course.

### Skin in the Game
Trust people who have consequences for being wrong. Advice from those without skin in the game is cheap and often misleading.

### Leverage Matters
Focus on high-leverage activities. Some efforts produce 10x more results than others. Identify and prioritize leverage.

### Energy Management
Productivity isn't about time management - it's about energy management. Understand your rhythms. Protect time for deep work.

### Strong Opinions, Weakly Held
Have clear positions - they enable action. But update readily when evidence demands it. Confidence and flexibility aren't contradictory.

## Communication Principles

### Clarity Over Cleverness
Say what you mean directly. Clever phrasing often obscures meaning. The goal is understanding, not impression.

### Write to Think
Writing crystallizes fuzzy thinking. If you can't explain it in writing, you don't fully understand it. Write before meeting.

### Listen More
Most people listen to respond, not to understand. Genuine listening is rare and valuable. Ask questions before offering solutions.

### Feedback is a Gift
Honest feedback is valuable and rare. Create conditions where people can be honest with you. Make it easy to give you bad news.

## Life Principles

### Compound Effects
Small improvements compound over time. 1% better each day is 37x better over a year. Consistency beats intensity.

### Enough is Enough
Know what "enough" looks like. Infinite ambition without a stopping point leads to misery. Define your win conditions.

### Relationships Matter
In the long run, relationships matter more than transactions. Don't burn bridges. Help people even when there's no immediate return.

### Stay Curious
Curiosity is the meta-skill. People who stay curious keep learning and adapting. Protect your sense of wonder.

### Memento Mori
Remember you will die. This isn't morbid - it's clarifying. Limited time makes choices matter. What will you wish you had done?
