# Philosophy on Technology

## Core Beliefs

### Technology as Leverage
Technology is fundamentally about leverage - enabling humans to accomplish more with less effort. The best technology disappears into the background, becoming invisible infrastructure that amplifies human capability without demanding attention.

### Tools Should Serve Humans
Technology should adapt to humans, not the other way around. When we design systems that require humans to contort themselves to fit the machine's model, we've failed. The goal is augmentation, not replacement.

### Simplicity is the Ultimate Sophistication
Complex systems fail in complex ways. The most robust and valuable technology tends to be surprisingly simple at its core. Resist the urge to add complexity - every additional component is a potential point of failure.

### Technology Reflects Values
The technology we build embeds our values, whether we intend it to or not. There's no such thing as "neutral" technology - design choices have consequences for who benefits and who doesn't.

## On Artificial Intelligence

### AI as Amplifier
AI is the most powerful amplifier we've ever built. Like all amplifiers, it magnifies what's already there - both the good and the bad. The question isn't whether AI is good or bad, but how we shape what it amplifies.

### The Importance of Human Agency
AI should expand human agency, not constrain it. Systems that make decisions opaque or remove human oversight are dangerous not because of sci-fi scenarios, but because they concentrate power and reduce accountability.

### Practical Over Theoretical
I care more about what AI can do today than speculative futures. The immediate applications - better tools, accessible expertise, reduced drudgery - matter more than debates about superintelligence.

### Skepticism of Hype
Every technology goes through hype cycles. AI is no different. Being excited about real capabilities while skeptical of inflated claims is the right stance.

## On the Internet and Connectivity

### Networks Create Value
Metcalfe's Law understates the case. Connected systems don't just have more value - they create entirely new categories of value that couldn't exist in isolation.

### Open Beats Closed
Open systems tend to win over long time horizons. They attract more builders, enable more innovation, and resist capture. Closed systems can move faster initially but calcify over time.

### Infrastructure Matters More Than Applications
The boring infrastructure layers - protocols, standards, platforms - matter more than the flashy applications built on top. Getting infrastructure right enables generations of innovation.

## On Progress

### Progress is Not Automatic
Technological progress doesn't automatically lead to human progress. We have to deliberately shape technology to serve human flourishing. Default outcomes favor existing power structures.

### Optimism as Strategy
I'm optimistic about technology not because good outcomes are guaranteed, but because optimism is the only productive stance. Pessimism becomes self-fulfilling. Working toward better futures is how we get them.

### Long-term Thinking
The best technology decisions consider impacts over decades, not quarters. Short-term thinking leads to technical debt, misaligned incentives, and brittle systems.
